Talent Intelligence.

Executive search is sold as one package. Most in-house teams only need part of it. We run the intake, map the market and write the outreach. You send it, run the process and make the hire.

The argument

A search fee pays for six jobs. You are probably only short on two.

A retained fee covers defining the role, researching the market, writing and sending the approach, screening candidates, running the process and closing the offer. Priced as one number they look like a single service. They are not.

In most in-house teams the gap is not process management. Teams that hire well already know how to interview, assess and close — usually better than an external firm, because they know their own business. What they run short of is research capacity and reach: a defensible view of who exists, and a message that gets a reply from someone who was not looking to move.

You are not paying a search firm to make calls. You are paying for the research that tells you who to call, and the reason they take the call.

That is the part we sell on its own.

What we do

  • Calibrate the role and test it against the market
  • Map the addressable market by name, not by job-title search
  • Tier every target and say why they are in or out
  • Report what the market says about pay, supply and competitor hiring
  • Write the outreach, per person, and the sequence that follows it
  • Brief your team before you go live

What you do

  • Send the outreach from your own name and domain
  • Handle replies and hold the first conversations
  • Screen, interview and assess
  • Run your own process and timelines
  • Negotiate and close the offer
  • Own the relationship from first contact onward
The deliverables

Three working documents, not a slide deck.

Built to be used by your team on the Monday after handover, and all of them stay yours.

One · from intake

The Role Kickoff Form

The whole search written down in one place before anyone is contacted. Nine sections, completed with you and signed off by you.

Sections one to five

  1. Hiring approval and commitmentApproved, funded and genuinely supported — or not yet ready to search.
  2. Compensation, equity and work modelRange, equity, office pattern, location and visa reality, vetted before mapping starts.
  3. Scorecard: mission and outcomesWhat this person must deliver at 60 days, 6 months and 12 months, each with a measure.
  4. Role, team and organisation contextThe actual job, who they work with, and the environment they are walking into.
  5. Candidate profile and search signalsBackgrounds that work, strengths worth chasing, dealbreakers and acceptable trade-offs.

Sections six to nine

  1. Candidate sell plan and realistic previewWhy an A-player would join, and the hard truths they should hear before they accept.
  2. Interview process and assessmentEach stage, who runs it, what it assesses and what decision it produces.
  3. Market mapping and sourcing strategyTalent pools, channels, comparable titles, companies to avoid and the indicative funnel.
  4. Final calibrationWhat a strong hire looks like, what would stop an offer, and real benchmark people to test the bar against.

Section six is the one people skip. Writing down the hard truths before you go to market is uncomfortable. It is also why strong candidates stay in at offer stage instead of withdrawing when reality arrives.

Two · the research

The Market Map

A live working file, not a static list. It is the tracker your search runs on until the hire is made.

The fit rating does the work. Everyone is scored 1 to 4 with a written reason. Only 3s and 4s go to outreach. The 1s and 2s stay in the file with the reason they were ruled out, so nobody re-argues them in week six.

The search kit behind it

Handed over so your team keeps finding people after we step back: target titles in three tiers, the titles to actively exclude and why, a target-company map segmented by relevance including off-limits firms, and the filter stack that produced the map.

Why hand over the search strings? A map has a shelf life; a method does not. Six months later your team can rerun the same search themselves. That is the point of unbundling.

Three · the engagement layer

The Outreach Sequence

A three-step cadence, and a first message written individually for every person you are approaching.

StepWhenWhat it doesPractical note
Message 1Day 0 Personal and specific. Why this person, one line on the business, and a small ask: a short, no-pressure conversation. Written individually for every target at or above the bar. No links, because links suppress reply rates.
Message 2Day 3–4, no reply A warm nudge that reaffirms the fit in one line and offers the detail. Persistence without pressure. The role overview goes here, and to anyone who replies with interest. One template.
Message 3Day 8–10, no reply A gracious close that leaves the door open. No guilt, no false urgency. The message that protects your reputation in the market whether or not this hire lands.

Message one is the whole point. Written one at a time, referencing what that individual has actually done, and sent from your name rather than an agency's. That is the difference between an approach a senior person answers and one they archive. Messages two and three are templates, because they should be.

When not to

Four situations where you should buy a full search instead.

Saying this plainly saves both of us a poor engagement.

  • 01
    Nobody internally has time to send the outreach and manage replies. A market map that never gets used is an expensive document. If the person meant to run this already has a full week, the work stalls in week two.
  • 02
    The approach has to come from a third party. Replacing someone still in post. Approaching a direct competitor where your name on the message creates a commercial or legal problem. Testing a market discreetly. These need an intermediary, which is the opposite of what unbundling does.
  • 03
    You need one party accountable end to end. Unbundled work carries no replacement guarantee, because we do not control the assessment or the decision. If your board expects one name accountable for the outcome, that is a full search engagement with the fee to match.
  • 04
    Identification is easy and persuasion is the hard part. In some markets everyone already knows the fifteen people who can do the job. There the value is a trusted relationship and a long conversation, not a map. We will tell you when that is the case.
Fair objections

The questions we are asked.

Is this just a list of names?

A list is names. A map is a calibrated view of a market: who is in scope and why, who was ruled out and why, how deep the pool is, what people are realistically paid, and where the risk sits.

The outreach is what turns it into conversations. Anyone can buy a database seat and export a list. Very few teams have time to write a genuinely personal approach to sixty senior people, which is why generic outreach gets generic response rates.

Do you guarantee we will make the hire?

No, and be careful of anyone who does. Nobody controls whether a specific candidate says yes.

What we are accountable for is the quality and coverage of the map, the accuracy of the intelligence in it, and outreach worth sending. If the market does not hold what you are looking for, you hear that at the intelligence stage rather than after twelve weeks.

What response rate should we expect?

It depends on the role, the market, the strength of your proposition and, more than anything, on how consistently the sequence is followed. We will not quote a number we cannot stand over.

What we will do is brief whoever is sending, review the first tranche of replies with you, and adjust the messaging if it is not landing. Most weak response rates come from stopping after one message.

What happens to the map once the search is over?

It is yours. Use it for the next hire in the same discipline. There is no introduction fee on anyone in it, and we do not claim ownership of candidates identified through your search.

That is a meaningful difference from a retained engagement, where the research usually stays with the firm and you pay again next time.

Can we start unbundled and move to a full search if it stalls?

Yes. The intake and the map carry over, so you are not paying twice for the same research. We agree the commercial treatment of that at the outset rather than negotiating it under pressure.

Talent Advisory.

Getting the role right before it reaches a market, capacity when the need is ongoing, and a full retained search when the approach has to come from a third party.

What it is

Most senior hires are decided before the first interview.

Not by choosing the wrong person, but by writing a role the market cannot supply. The brief describes an ideal. The market has people. Closing that gap is unglamorous work, and it is usually the reason a search lands or drifts.

So we define the role against what exists, set a scorecard that can actually be evidenced, band the compensation honestly, and design a process you can describe to your board without ringing us first.

If the band on the table will not clear the market, you hear it in week one rather than week nine. If the role is really two roles, we say so before anyone is approached.

About.

Someone who has hired at this level, not a firm that resells research.

How we work

Three things we will not trade.

01

Candour over comfort

If the band will not clear the market, you hear it in week one. If the role is really two roles, we say so before anyone is approached. It is an awkward ten minutes that saves a year.

02

Evidence sharpens judgement

It never replaces it. Data tells you who exists, what they cost and how a market is moving. It cannot tell you whether someone holds their nerve in a hard quarter. We gather the evidence properly, then give you a view.

03

No guarantees we cannot stand over

Nobody controls whether a specific candidate says yes. We are accountable for the coverage of the map, the accuracy of the intelligence, and outreach worth sending — and we will say when the market does not hold what you want.

On AI

It speeds up the research. It does not do the judging.

AI helps structure the intelligence and gets through the volume faster. It does not decide who belongs in a map, judge whether someone is a credible fit, or write anything we would not put our name to.

The reason the outreach works is that a person who has hired at this level wrote it, one message at a time, referencing what that individual has actually done. Volume-generated messaging is precisely what senior people have learned to ignore.
